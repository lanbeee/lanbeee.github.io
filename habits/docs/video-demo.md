## The pitch

**Tell Tings what matters, and what it needs. Let it work out how it fits. Then know what to do next.**

The emotional promise: spend less of your life coordinating the life you want to live.

The recurring visual is a simple wish becoming an actual place in the day. A walk in daylight. A call when family is available. An errand while already across town. Those wishes become one agenda alongside meetings, meals, work, prayer, and travel.

Lead with the resulting experience. Show configuration briefly after the viewer wants the result. Spoken wishes below are narration; they do not imply a natural-language input feature.

### A day worth making room for

A walk before dark. Finish the report. Groceries. Call home. All reasonable things. So why does the day fill up before they happen? Your calendar, forecast, and to-do list each know part of the story. Tings brings it together: what matters, what fits, and what to do next.

### Tell it how your life works

Start with the things that keep coming back. Read daily. Call home weekly. Laundry every seven days. Set each rhythm once; log what you do, and it continues. You choose the breathing room. Laundry can happen a day early or two days later. Another routine stays firm. Tings works with the flexibility you allow. And flexibility can mean options. The same workout could happen before work, at lunch, or in the evening. Meetings rule out the first two today. Evening fits. One workout, one chosen time. Link the shower directly after the workout, and make room for the whole routine. Time for exercise includes time to be ready afterwards. Other windows can follow what actually happened. Breakfast ran late? A routine set thirty minutes after its logged completion moves with it.

### Make room for that walk

Now bring in your device calendar. The meetings are already commitments. Tings plans the flexible parts around them, instead of asking you to juggle two separate pictures. For our family walk, I want thirty minutes before sunset, preferably dry. Daylight, weather, and calendar space need to overlap. Here it is: meeting, then walk, with daylight left. And the window follows sunset through the seasons, without rewriting a clock time. Your version might be a cooler run, gardening out of the wind, or a picnic when the forecast is right. Temperature, rain, wind, feels-like, UV, air quality: choose the conditions that matter. Changing times work for other routines too: gardening after sunrise, or prayer through the year. The day can follow these anchors alongside your calendar.

### When the day asks you to choose

Configure meetings to count toward your work goal. Two hours of meetings can leave four hours of a six-hour day. Now fit the remaining work into useful sessions around those commitments. For a project, set its duration, deadline, and minimum session size. Progress needn't wait for one enormous gap. And that haircut? Set Plan by Friday, with room to choose an earlier day. Log it Thursday, and the one-off aim clears. The regular rhythm continues. When two things want the same gap, your priorities help decide. Here, the report matters more than paperwork. Now consider two equally important routines: the call home you've postponed, and tidying that's only just due. Overdue things deserve attention too. Tings weighs those signals alongside commitments and what actually fits. See your total free time and its biggest stretch. Two and a half free hours aren't necessarily together. A friend suggests Saturday, two until four. Could you make room? Preview moving a flexible errand, without changing the actual agenda. See whether things can move, spill later, or hit a fixed commitment before saying yes.

### One outing, fewer loose ends

Groceries still need doing. But a thirty-minute errand needs more than a thirty-minute gap when there's travel. The journey belongs in the plan. Groceries can happen at either store. One is across town; the other is near today's appointment. With suitable hours and time to shop, Tings can choose the nearby one. And that library book? An optional errand can become relevant near a planned visit. Same outing, another opportunity, if there's room. The choice becomes something I can follow: appointment, four-minute journey, groceries. Include the trip home too, so the outing fits the rest of the day.

### Life changes. Keep going.

Of course, life doesn't follow the first draft. Miss a session, or add an appointment: Tings can rebuild around the time that remains. Flexible work moves where it can; tight days still require choices. On Android, enabled reminders follow the published agenda. When the walk moves, its reminder can move too. Choose notifications or ringing alarms, for starts, ends, departures, or arrivals. A last-chance warning can tell you the walk's opportunity is shrinking. And a departure reminder includes the journey: arrive at three, leave at two forty-five. The useful nudge is when you need to act. Open directions straight from Tings. Or attach a chat shortcut to calling home, and a mail link to an email session. Put the starting point beside the intention.

### Get on with your day

Repeating routines. Possible times and places. Weather, priorities, overdue things, and commitments. All those messy options become one agenda. I can spend less effort deciding, and more time doing. Tings is free. No ads, no account. Your list is stored and planned on your device. Network features, such as maps and weather, contact services for their job. And now it's time for that walk. We made room for it. I can put the phone away, and go be there. Tings. Make room for what matters.

## How to make the magic visible

- **Use one recurring hero:** the walk. Introduce it as something the viewer wants, show the planner finding time, disturb the day, and end on the walk actually happening.
- **Reveal combinations:** sunlight plus weather plus meetings plus travel plus an alarm. Let the viewer see those constraints resolve into one clear action.
- **Give every surprise a before/after:** meeting moved → flexible row changes; six-hour goal → meeting credit → remaining work; place alternatives → selected venue; changed agenda → updated alarm.
- **Make wishes sound human:** say “some daylight with my family.” Show the relevant controls for a few seconds after the resulting plan has earned attention.
- **Hold on the result:** calendar events, travel, and the activity must be visible together long enough to understand the fit. Record the settled agenda.
- **Keep ordinary life in frame:** shoes by the door, a grocery bag, a parent answering a call, a laptop closing. The app is the bridge between intention and action.
- **Sound:** a notification becomes the cue to pick up shoes or leave the house. End in the activity, with the phone put away.
- **Choose a few surprises for the main film:** the library above supports follow-ups. Narration should leave room to see each result.

## Recording setup and factual guardrails

This is a sales script and shot plan with rendered illustrated animatics; it is not a verified live demo session. Personal items in the October 7, 2026 backup inspired the work, family, prayer, grooming, and maintenance examples. No live items or personal backup files were changed.

Prepare a separate demo profile with generic places and names. Use Android for device-calendar and phone-alarm scenes. Prepare these specific demonstrations before filming:

1. A 30-minute walk inside a sunset-relative allowed window, with a weather profile and imported fixed meetings. Use actual available forecast conditions. If also using a park, configure its location and travel and verify the resulting schedule.
2. Two dates with different actual sunrise/sunset times, showing the same stored relative rule resolving to different clocks.
3. A refreshed calendar change that visibly affects a flexible row without moving the fixed event itself.
4. A six-hour breakable work goal with two hours of non-overlapping imported meeting credit. Select only relevant work calendars/events: configured imported credit is not automatic semantic classification of meetings.
5. A fixed appointment, nearby errand, alternative venue, opening hours, and travel estimates that yield the route being narrated. Route-aware planning does not guarantee the globally shortest whole-day route or provide live traffic/navigation.
6. A chosen enabled Android reminder before and after a published agenda change. Use a real ringing-alarm recording and actual drop-warning text. Permission setup belongs outside the main sales sequence.

Relative windows and forecast guidance can be combined, but they cannot guarantee suitable weather or an available slot. Priorities, explicit plans, and certain linked/critical rows can override weather rejection. The film should demonstrate an ordinary flexible example.

A drop warning concerns the estimated loss of a feasible opportunity; it is not synonymous with the suggested start or a guaranteed deadline. Keep “estimated” in the voiceover or visible context.

Android reminders are reconciled to the published agenda. Closed-app refresh is optional, runs roughly every 15–30 minutes, and may be delayed by Android. Avoid a claim that all background changes are detected instantly. Do not imply cross-device data synchronization or automatic weather-based transport-mode switching.
